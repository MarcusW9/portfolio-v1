Launching a marketplace inside a 50-year-old retailer

Argos had never sold third-party stock. I led the programme that gave it a seller platform, a catalogue model and a new commission revenue line.

£12m+GMV since launch
£1m+commission revenue
20knew SKUs on Argos channels
25+KYC-approved sellers before go-live
Role
Lead PM, Argos Marketplace
Timeline
Jan 2025 – present
Team
5+ delivery teams, platform & integration vendors, director-level sponsors
Stack
Mirakl, iPaaS integration, internal APIs

The problem

Growing range without holding more stock meant building capabilities Argos had never run: seller onboarding, product ingestion, marketplace orders and payouts. The investment case ran past £20m, and five-plus teams each owned a piece of the flow.

The strategy

  • Wrote the procurement RFP and led vendor evaluation, landing on Mirakl as the platform.
  • Pushed iPaaS integration over a long internal build to get sellers connected sooner.
  • Ran workshops on data ownership for each core flow, then cut the MVP to what launch needed.

Trade-offs

Buying the platform gave up some control of the roadmap in exchange for launch speed. Taxonomy was the one area we did not compromise on, because bad category data blocks search, browse and every later seller.

Execution

Cost estimates across vendors for director-level business cases, API schema and system-behaviour alignment with Engineering and Architecture, and coordinated testing across downstream systems.

What I took from it

In a marketplace, the catalogue and data-ownership model is the product. Settling who owns each field early saved us more time than any single feature did.

Build, don't buy: a partner platform in six weeks

As a founding member of Argos Labs, Argos UK's new AI-led delivery team, I replaced 800 spreadsheet rows with an in-house partner platform in six weeks.

£300Kestimated engineering cost avoided
6 wksfrom brief to working MVP
800partner records moved off spreadsheets
£0licence fees, fully owned in-house
Role
Senior PM; founding member of Argos Labs, alongside my Marketplace role
Timeline
~6 weeks to MVP, then iteration & handover
Team
1 engineer, plus onboarding, ops & compliance teams
Stack
v0, Claude, AI-generated UI, workflow engine

The problem

Argos Labs is a small team Argos UK set up to solve business problems quickly with AI-led development. I joined as a founding member alongside my Marketplace role. Hundreds of seller submissions arrived through forms and landed in large spreadsheets. Nobody clearly owned each stage, onboarding steps were missed, and compliance tracking was manual. Commercial PRM quotes ran to hundreds of thousands of pounds with long integration timelines.

The hypothesis

One PM and one engineer, using AI for wireframes, screens and code, could ship a fit-for-purpose PRM faster and cheaper than a licence, and prove the Argos Labs operating model at the same time.

What we built

  • Separate pipelines for acquisition, onboarding and account management, each with stages and owners.
  • Progression rules and warnings that stop a seller skipping a compliance step.
  • A single seller record holding data, files, notes and full history.

Trade-offs

No licence meant no vendor support, so ownership and handover were agreed with the receiving team during the build, not after it. Reporting was designed into the data model and left for the next phase.

What I took from it

A well-structured prompt is now a PRD you can run. The harder PM work was getting operations to agree on one workflow before we generated any screens.

Turning a free parents' app into a business

YoungPlanet lets parents pass on outgrown children's items to families nearby. I owned the product end to end: growing the community, finding ways to earn from it, and taking it abroad.

160k+app downloads
35k+items listed across the UK
40kemployees reached through the Travis Perkins deal
2European markets launched
Role
Product Lead, reporting to the founders
Timeline
Oct 2022 – Jan 2025
Team
Founders, engineering and design
Product
Consumer mobile app, in-app ad space, B2B offer

The problem

The app grew because it was free and useful, but the circular model left little to charge parents for. The business needed income that didn't put a paywall between families.

The strategy

  • Built the infrastructure for in-app ad space, which secured a £12k time-bound contract.
  • Designed a B2B offer on top of the consumer app, signing Travis Perkins and its 40k employees.
  • Launched in two European markets, covering translation, comms and GDPR.

Trade-offs

Every revenue idea was tested against one question: does it make giving and getting items any harder? Ads and employer deals passed. Charging parents did not.

Ownership

Strategy, monetisation and the full product lifecycle, working day to day with the founders from ideation through to development.

What I took from it

When users won't pay, find who benefits from them being there. Employers wanted a family perk, and the community was already built.

From pre-revenue to 20,000 landlords and tenants

A PropTech start-up for DIY landlords with under 1,000 users and no revenue. In eleven months we shipped its first paid product and grew twentyfold.

20×users, <1k to 20k in 11 months
+20%conversion on deposit replacement
500property listings from zero
2×user base in Q1 2021 from SEO work
Role
Junior PM, second product hire
Timeline
Oct 2020 – Aug 2021
Team
CEO, Senior PM, design, offshore dev team
Stack
BigQuery, GA, GTM, Mixpanel, Heap, Intercom, Figma

The problem

Landlords liked the free tools but the company earned nothing from them. We needed a first paid product that fitted how self-managing landlords already worked, and enough traffic to prove it.

The strategy

  • Deposit Replacement Scheme with spread payments across the year, a clear difference from competitors. Required FCA approval and financial integrations.
  • Maintenance marketplace: tenants report issues, landlords book a tradesperson in-app, we take commission.
  • SEO changes that doubled the user base in a quarter.

How we optimised

A/B tests, user interviews and BigQuery funnel analysis on the deposit flow lifted conversion by 20%. I also rebuilt support triage in Jira, which cut ticket response times sharply.

Collaboration

Ran sprints and prioritisation for the dev team, worked with the CEO on vision, and co-presented investor demos, role-playing landlord and tenant journeys.

What I took from it

The first revenue product should sit where money already changes hands. Deposits and repairs were payments landlords made anyway, so we charged for making them easier.
